Dissection of the mouse sections. Each row is one tissue. (A) The full mouse section, coloured by EBCC label; the rectangle marks the dissected region used for the main (dissected) datasets, and cells outside it are faded. (B) The human sample, coloured the same way; labels below 1% of cells in the human sample, the dissected region and the full section are pooled as Other. The dissection keeps the mouse compartment that corresponds to the human sample and removes tissue the human sample lacks: in bone marrow, the epiphyses, cartilage and cortical stroma (MSC 20.8% to 7.4% of cells; T cells and NK/ILC that lie on cartilage 2.2% to 0.1% and 3.3% to 0.5%); in breast cancer, the normal mammary gland, fat, lymph-node-like tissue and capsule around the tumour (normal epithelium 15.3% to 12.3%); in colon, a region of the colon wall; in fetal liver, one liver lobe. Differences in composition that remain reflect the biology of the samples, for example the erythroid-rich neonatal mouse marrow, and are not removed. In breast cancer, 14 cells inside the rectangle were dropped at concatenation because they had no counts on the shared genes.
